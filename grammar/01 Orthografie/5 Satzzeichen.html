Satzzeichen
  • The Point (.) is at the end of a statement and in many abbreviations.
    Le train arrive.
    The train arrives.
    S.N.C.F.
    National company of French railways
  • The comma (,) separates parts of sentences from each other. No comma however stands before necessary Relative rates and by auxiliaries with que einzuleiten.
    Pendant les vacances, nous sommes à la mer.
    We're at the sea during the holidays.
    Voilà le livre que je te donne.
    Here's the book I'm giving you.
    Je pense que tu as raison.
    I think you're right.
  • The question mark (?) is at the end of a question.
    Tu viens me voir ?
    Besuchst du mich?
  • The exclamation mark (!) Follows a call or command.
    Au secours !
    Help!
    À votre santé !
    To your health!
  • The quotes («)"Guillemets" are used for quotations or direct speech.
    On va dîner au « Cheval Blanc ».
    We're having dinner at the Cheval Blanc.
  • The indent (–) In a dialogue, the speaker changes.
    – Tu prends du thé ?
    – Non, merci.
    – Nimmst du Tee?
    – Nein, danke.

In French, there is a narrow space in front of ?, !, :, and the guillemets («»).
