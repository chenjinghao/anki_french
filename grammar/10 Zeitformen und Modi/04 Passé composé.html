Passé composé

The passé composé is a composite past form. It describes a completed action in the past, which often still has an impact on the present.

The passé composé is made similar to the German Perfect: Present of Hilfsverben être (his) or avoir (have) + participe passé.

j’ai je suis je me suis
tu as tu es tu t’es
il a il est il s’est
nous avons nous sommes nous nous sommes
vous avez vous êtes vous vous êtes
ils ont ils sont ils se sont

The following verbs form the passé composé with être:

  • Movement and state morphology aller, arriver, entrer, partir, rester, rentrer, tomber, venir and revenir.
    • Aujourd'hui, je suis allé(e) au musée. Je suis arrivé(e) vers midi.
    Today I went to the museum. I arrived around noon.
  • The verbs naître, devenir, mourir and décéder.
    • L'artiste est né en 1985.
    The artist was born in 1985.

All reflexive verbs form the passé composé with être.

Paul s'est réveillé tôt ce matin.
Paul woke up this morning.
Emma et Léo se sont promenés dans le parc.
Emma and Léo went for a walk in the park.

Some verbs can use the passé composé both with avoir and être bilden: monter, descendre, sortir, entrer and rentrer.

  • Usually with être:
    • Il est monté dans sa chambre.
    He went up to his room.
  • With avoirif a direct object follows:
    • Elle a monté les courses au troisième étage.
    She carried the purchases up to the third floor.

Usage

The passé composé is the most common past form in French.

It describes an action that lies before the time of speech, but which still works until the present. Often a time determination is given.

Isabelle a obtenu son diplôme le mois dernier.
Isabelle graduated last month.
L'invention de l'imprimerie a révolutionné la diffusion du savoir.
The invention of printing has revolutionized the dissemination of knowledge.

It is used for successive actions or limited repetitions:

Ce matin, elle s'est réveillée, a pris son petit-déjeuner, puis est partie travailler.
She woke up this morning, had breakfast and then went to work.
Hier soir, mon téléphone a vibré cinq fois.
Last night, my phone vibrated five times.

It expresses the pretemporaneity of an event that precedes another present event or state in time.

Marc s'est foulé la cheville en faisant du jogging et il ne peut pas participer à la course aujourd'hui.
Marc sprained his ankle while jogging and can't take part in the race today.

In the spoken language it often replaces the passé simple:

Victor Hugo est né à Besançon en 1802.
Victor Hugo was born in Besançon in 1802.

Passé surcomposé

The passé surcomposé is used very rarely nowadays. It serves to describe an action that was completed before another past action. plus-que-parfait, which also expresses pre-timeliness, emphasizes the passé surcomposé more the conclusion of the action before the main action. Examples of use are mainly found in southern France and are often considered dialectal. In the written language it is hardly used anymore, and many modern speakers are no longer aware of its function and form.

It will be passé composé from avoir/être + participe passé gebildet.

j’ai eu je suis eu je me suis eu
tu as eu tu es eu tu t’es eu
il a eu il est eu il s’est eu
nous avons eu nous sommes eu nous nous sommes eu
vous avez eu vous êtes eu vous vous êtes eu
ils ont eu ils sont eu ils se sont eu
J'ai eu mangé.
(heutzutage eher: J'avais mangé.)
I had eaten. (literally: I had eaten.)
Nous avons été partis.
(heutzutage eher: Nous étions partis.)
We had gone away. (literally, we had gone away.)

It often stands in the secondary sentence after time conjunctions:

Dès qu'il a eu terminé son discours, le public a applaudi.
As soon as he finished his speech, the audience applauded.

With the passé composé In the main sentence, it expresses the immediate pre-timeliness of a closed event.
