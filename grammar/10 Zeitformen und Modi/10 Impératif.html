Impératif

The impératif (Imperative) is used to express a direct request, wish, command, request, recommendation, prohibition or advice.

In French there is the imperative only for three people: 2. person singular, 1. person plural and 2. person plural.

French imperative sentences usually end with a point. An exclamation mark is only used in particularly strong requests.

The imperative forms in the plural usually correspond to the indicative-present forms. Exception: The 2nd person singular of the ‑er‑ verbs and some ‑ir‑ verb ends on ‑e.

If the imperative is the pronoun en or y without the following Infinitiv follow, the imperative receives the ending ‑esto facilitate the debate, e.g. penses-y (denk daran).

The imperative expresses, among other things, a desire, an instruction, an order or a advice.

Profite bien de tes vacances.
Enjoy your vacation!
Mélangez les ingrédients pendant 5 minutes.
Mix the ingredients for five minutes.
Écoute attentivement !
Listen carefully!
Partons maintenant !
Let's go now!

donc emphasizes the imperative.

Essayez donc cette recette.
Try this recipe!

Instead of the non-existent 1st person singular, sometimes the 1st person plural is used.

Je me suis dit : restons calme.
I said to myself: Stay calm!

For commands to a third person (singular or plural), the subjonctif is used.

Qu'elle vienne tout de suite !
Tell her to come right now!
