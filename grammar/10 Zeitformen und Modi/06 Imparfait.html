Imparfait

The imparfait is a simple past form. It is used to describe backgrounds and to describe situations. imparfait expresses states and actions that are not strictly limited in time.

The imparfait from the verb stem of the 1st person plural present derived.

je j’ je me je m’
tu tu te tu t’
il il se il s’
nous nous nous
vous vous vous
ils ils se ils s’

The French imparfait is used in the following situations:

Actions without time limits

The imparfait describes ongoing actions, backgrounds or persistent states in the past, without mentioning the beginning or end.

Les enfants jouaient dans le parc.
The children played in the park.
Sophie semblait fatiguée et avait l'air préoccupée.
Sophie seemed tired and worried.

Past habits

The imparfait expresses regular actions or habits in the past.

Chaque été, nous allions à la montagne.
Every summer we went to the mountains.

Courtesy of expression

The imparfait is also used for polite formulations.

Je voulais vous demander un service.
I wanted to ask you a favor.

Hypothetical situations

After the conjunction si (if) the imparfaitto express a condition not yet fulfilled.

Si j'avais plus de temps, j'apprendrais le japonais.
If I had more time, I'd learn Japanese.

The imparfait is often used in combination with the passé composé used to represent several types of action in the past.

Simultaneous actions in the past

The passé composé describes completed actions, while the imparfait expresses the background or persistent conditions.

Il faisait beau quand nous sommes arrivés à la plage.
It was nice weather when we arrived at the beach.
Lorsque Pierre est entré, Marie lisait un livre.
When Pierre came in, Marie was reading a book.

Timely events / indefinite states in the past

Je ne savais pas qu'il était malade.
(andauernder Zustand im imparfait)
I didn't know he was sick.
Je ne l’ai appris qu'il y a deux jours.
(bestimmtes, abgeschlossenes Ereignis im passé composé)
I only found out two days ago.

habit / repetition in the past

Enfants, ils passaient toujours leurs vacances au bord de la mer. Ils sont allés plusieurs fois à Carnac, en Bretagne.
In childhood they always went on holiday by the sea. They were several times in Carnac in Brittany.

In the first sentence, the imparfait a time-limited habit in the past. In the second sentence, the passé composé a time-limited repetition (several times) in the past.
