The Infinitive

The infinitive is the basic form of the verb. It is not conjugated, so it does not indicate either person or number. In French, the infinitive is very versatile and more frequently used than in German, often as a noun or in its pure verb function.

Forms

One distinguishes two forms of the infinitive: the infinitive present and the infinitive perfect. Both can be in the active and in the Passiv stehen.

Infinitive Present Infinitiv Perfekt
Aktiv
chanter, partir
singen, weggehen
avoir chanté, être parti(e)(s)
have sung, gone away
Passiv
être chanté(e)(s)
to be sung
avoir été chanté(e)(s)
have been sung

In denying the infinitive, both negation elements (ne ... pas) before the infinitive (present) or before the Hilfsverb (Perfekt).

Il est important de ne pas oublier les clés.
It's important to have the keys not to vergessen.
Elle regrette de ne pas avoir pu venir.
She regrets not have come can.

The Infinitive as a Noun

Like a noun, the infinitive can have the function of a subject, object, or predictive complement.

  • As subject:
    Voyager ouvre l'esprit.
    Reisen Open the mind.
  • As object:
    Il déteste attendre.
    He hates it too warten.
  • As a predictive supplement:
    Partir, c'est mourir un peu.
    Going away means going a little too sterben.

Some infinitives have become fixed nouns in speech: le dîner (dinner), le déjeuner (the lunch), le devoir (the obligation), le pouvoir (power), le sourire (The smile).

The Infinitive as Verb

In its function as a verb, the infinitive can be determined by adverbs or additions.

Il faut apprendre à conduire prudemment.
You have to learn, vorsichtig to drive.
Elle est capable de travailler avec ambition.
She's capable of... with ambition to work.

The Infinitive can also express commands, questions or exclamations:

Agiter avant emploi.
Before use Shake. (Befehl)
Que faire ?
Was tun? (Frage)

The Infinitive by Verbs

The infinitive is very common as Supplement to another verb used. Depending on the verb, the infinitive is without preposition, with à or with de. Since there are no simple rules for this, the connection must be learned for each verb.
