À moi or le mien?

The constructions "à moi" and "le mien" can be used similarly in many situations, but have subtle differences:

  • "C'est à moi": More informal and emotional. Often used to claim something that the owner is not clear. Can express pride or greed for possession.
    • Tout ça, c'est à moi !
    This all belongs to mir!
  • "C'est le mien": Descriptive and often implies that the object has been in possession for a long time. It fits better with questions where the object has been described.
    • - C'est la voiture de ta mère ?
    - Non, c'est la mienne.
    Ist das das Auto deiner Mutter?
    - Nein, das ist meins.

In most cases, both forms are interchangeable. "C'est à moi" is slightly less formal and the correct answer to the question "C'est à qui ça ?", while "C'est le mien" The object is the object that is the object of the object (e.g. "À qui est cette voiture ?").
