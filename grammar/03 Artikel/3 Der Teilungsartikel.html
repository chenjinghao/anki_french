The divisional article (de)

The divisional article (article partitif) is used to express an indeterminate amount of something that is not countable (e.g. water, courage) or its exact number is not relevant (e.g. fries). In German, there is often no article for this.

It is derived from the preposition de and the certain Articles gebildet.

Usage

The division article is used:

  • In the singular for uncountable things and abstract terms:
    Tu veux de la soupe ?
    Would you like some soup?
    Il faut de la patience pour ce travail.
    This work requires patience.
  • In the plural for an indefinite number of counting things:
    J'ai acheté des pommes au marché.
    I bought apples at the market.

After a Verneinung the division articles shall be: du, de la, de l', des to de (or d’ before vowel/mute h).

Je bois du café. → Je ne bois pas de café.
I drink coffee. I don't drink coffee.
Elle a de la chance. → Elle n'a jamais de chance.
She's lucky. She's never lucky.

When an adjective is in the plural, it becomes the noun. des to de (or d’).

Nous avons des amis. → Nous avons de bons amis.
We have friends. We have good friends.

Exception: If adjective and noun form a fixed unit, remain des erhalten, z.B. des jeunes gens (junge Leute), des petits fours (small pastries).

Dividing articles for sport and music

For activities with: faire and instruments with: jouer the division article is also used:

En hiver, je fais du ski.
I ski in winter.
Ma sœur joue du violon.
Meine Schwester spielt Geige.
