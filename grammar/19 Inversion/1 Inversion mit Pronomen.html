Inversion with pronouns

In French and German, the subject usually stands before the verb, as in the case of vous êtes Both languages also know the inversion in which verb and subject exchange places: êtes-vous In German, the inversion is mainly used in questions and auxiliary sentences, in French it has several functions.

In French, a hyphen is always set between the inverted verb and subject:

vous savez → savez-vous
il est → est-il

So do the Liaison between Subjektpronomen and verbs are mandatory, it is also required between inverted verbs and subject pronouns.

When a verb ends on a vowel and il, elle or on ‑t- is inserted for better pronunciation:

il a → a-t‑il
elle parle → parle-t‑elle
on écoute → écoute-t‑on

Movements inserted

The inversion is also used in direct speech or comments.

"Je comprends", dit-il, "la situation".
"I understand," he said, "the situation."
Thomas est absent, paraît-il.
Thomas seems to be absent.
Il y a un malentendu, me semble-t-il.
There seems to be a misunderstanding.

Adverbs and adverbs

According to certain adverbs at the beginning of the sentence an inversion must follow.

  • à peine (kaum)
    À peine avait-il fini qu'il est parti.
    Kaum hatte er geendet, da ging er.
  • à plus forte raison (erst recht)
    À plus forte raison devez-vous étudier.
    Erst recht musst du lernen.
  • du moins (zumindest)
    Du moins peut-on essayer.
    At least you can try.
  • (et) encore (dennoch)
    Encore faut-il le dire.
    Still, you have to say it.
  • encore moins (even less)
    Encore moins pouvait-elle refuser.
    Even less could she refuse.
  • rarement (selten)
    Rarement ai-je vu un tel spectacle.
    I've rarely seen such a spectacle.

Optionale Inversion

For certain designs, the inversion is optional.

After peut-être, sans doute And the proclaiming combien you need either an inversion or the word que:

Peut-être viendra-t-il. / Peut-être qu'il viendra.
Maybe he'll come.
Sans doute sont-ils malades. / Sans doute qu'ils sont malades.
Surely they're sick.
Combien de livres a-t-il lus ! / Combien de livres qu'il a lus !
How many books he's read!

After three adverbs, the inversion is completely optional:

  • ainsi (so)
  • aussi (bien) (ebenso)
  • en vain (in vain)
Ainsi sont-ils venus. / Ainsi ils sont venus.
That's how they came.
En vain avons-nous attendu. / En vain nous avons attendu.
We waited in vain.

If the inversion is optional, both variants are equally correct, but the inversion sounds more formal.
