Inversion with nouns

The French inversion is not limited to pronouns – it also works with nouns and proper names, but is a bit more complicated. In some cases, the inversion with nouns is mandatory, in others optional.

Verpflichtende Inversion

1. Direkte Rede

In direct speech, the normal sequence of words must be: Subjekt + Verb to Verb + Subjekt inverted:

"Je pense", dit l'infirmière, "qu'il reste de la soupe".
"I think," said the nurse, "there's still soup."
"Je n'ai pas faim", répondit Marie.
"I'm not hungry," Marie replied.

2. Fragen

In the case of questions with a noun or name as a subject, this subject remains in its position and is repeated by a pronoun, which is then inverted with the verb.

La voiture est-elle en panne ?
Is the car broken?
Les étudiants ont-ils réussi l'examen ?
Did the students pass the exam?
Pierre cherche-t-il ses clés ?
Is Pierre looking for his keys?

This construction is grammatically correct. Alternatively, the less formal variant can be used with est-ce que verwenden.

Stylistic Inversion

In some cases, the inversion is optional and only serves the style level or formality.

1. According to relative pronouns

Voici le livre dont parlent Thomas et Sophie.
Voici le livre dont Thomas et Sophie parlent.
Here's the book Thomas and Sophie are talking about.

2. After a comparison

Il est plus intelligent que ne pensait son professeur.
Il est plus intelligent que son professeur ne pensait.
He's smarter than his teacher thought.

3. The Emphasis of the Subject

Especially in literature:

Brilla une étoile. / Une étoile brilla.
A star lit up.

No hyphen will be set for the inversion with nouns or names!
