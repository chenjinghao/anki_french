Informelle Verneinung

According to the standard rule, a French verb is used by ne before the verb and pas This is the correct French grammar, which should always be used in writing:

Je ne veux pas y aller.
I don't want to go.
On ne sait pas.
We don't know.
Ne touche pas !
Don't touch it!
Il n'y en a pas.
There is none.

The actual French pronunciation, however, looks different, as can be heard in films, on television and in native speakers.

Debates of the European Parliament ne

Theoretically, ne as a complete syllable \nə\ In reality, the vowel is almost never pronounced outside poetry:

Je n'veux pas y aller.
On n'sait pas.
N'touche pas !

Dismissal of ne

In the common language: ne komplett weggelassen.

Je veux pas y aller.
I don't want to go.
On sait pas.
We don't know.
Touche pas !
Don't touch it!
Il y en a pas.
There is none.

This also applies to other negation words:

Je n'aime plus cette émission. → J'aime plus cette émission.
I don't like this show anymore.
Il ne va jamais au ciné. → Il va jamais au ciné.
He never goes to the movies.

In this context, it is important to know this ubiquitous aspect of the pronunciation, even if you yourself are not aware of the ne That's how you speak in France.
