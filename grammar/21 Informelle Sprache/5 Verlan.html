Verlan

Verlan is a form of the French slang (argot), where the syllables of a word are exchanged to create a new, coded language. verlan It is the inversion of l'envers (the reverse).

Originally used by criminals in the 19th century to communicate unnoticed, Verlan became popular in the 1980s and 90s by teenagers in the suburbs of Paris and by hip-hop music. It serves to delimit and strengthen group identity. meuf or keuf have become so widespread that they have become part of the common language.

Wortbildung

The basic principle is the reversal of syllables. Since this is a primary oral phenomenon, the spelling is oriented towards pronunciation, which often leads to phonetic adaptations: words that are based on a mute consonant or a mute ‑e end, often get the sound after the reversal \œ\, written as ‑eu‑. Often the last vowel of the reversed form is omitted.
