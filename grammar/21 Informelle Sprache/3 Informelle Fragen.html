Informelle Fragen

In French, there are various ways to: Asking Questions. The official variants are the inversion (formal) and est-ce que (Standard language), but there are other possibilities in the common language.

1. Intonation

A statement becomes a question by raising the voice at the end of the sentence – just like in German:

Tu viens demain ? ↗
Are you coming tomorrow?
Il va nous aider ? ↗
Will he help us?
Vous voulez l'essayer ? ↗
Do you want to try it?

You can also add a reinsurance issue.

Tu viens demain, c’est ça ?
You're coming tomorrow, aren't you?
Il va nous aider, pas vrai ?
He's gonna help us, right?

2. Emphasis with question words

An interrogative pronoun or ‑adverb can be at the beginning or end of the question (more commonly in the end):

Quand t'es arrivé ? → T'es arrivé quand ?
When did you arrive?
À quoi tu penses ? → Tu penses à quoi ?
Woran denkst du?
Pourquoi tu dis ça ? → Tu dis ça pourquoi ?
Why do you say that?

3. C’est with question words

If you have questions for information about a noun, you can c’est before the question:

Quand est ton anniversaire ? → Ton anniversaire, c’est quand ?
When's your birthday?
Où est ton école ? → Ton école, c’est où ?
Where's your school?
Qui est ce mec ? → Ce mec, c’est qui ?
Who's this guy?
Quelle est ta couleur préférée ? → Ta couleur préférée, c’est quoi ?
What's your favorite color?

4. Qui c’est qui

Qui c’est qui replaced in the common language qui:

Qui c’est qui a fait ça ?
Who did this?
Qui c’est qui va conduire ?
Who's going to drive?

5. Elimination of est-ce

A very informal way of asking questions is to use only que statt est-ce que.

Quand qu'elle va arriver ? (Quand est-ce qu'elle va arriver ?)
When does she arrive?
Pourquoi que tu dis ça ? (Pourquoi est-ce que tu dis ça ?)
Why do you say that?
Où que tu vas ? (Où est-ce que tu vas ?)
Where are you going?
