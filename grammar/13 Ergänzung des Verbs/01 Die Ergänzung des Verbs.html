Complementing the verb (Valence)

French verbs can have different additions, similar to German verbs, for example:

  • a direktes Objekt:
    J'aime le chocolat.
    I like Schokolade.
  • a indirektes Objekt:
    Je parle à ma sœur.
    I speak with my sister.
  • one Infinitiv:
    Il doit travailler.
    He must: arbeiten.
  • one predictive supplement:
    Le ciel est bleu.
    The sky is blau.

The French and German verbs do not always agree on what can be connected, and these differences must be learned.

The Intransitive Verb

Intransitive verbs cannot connect an object. They form the sentence alone with the subject.

Le grand-père dort.
The Grandfather Sleeps.
La voiture roule vite.
The car is driving schnell.

The phenomenon described is called linguistics. Valenz. The valence determines how many and what additions a verb "requires or can allow. Verbs like aller or changer have a variable Valenz – they can change their supplementary structure and thus often get completely new meanings. Therefore, it is important to always learn French verbs with their possible valence frames.
