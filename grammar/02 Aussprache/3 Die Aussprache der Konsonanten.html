The Consonants' Debate

The consonants' pronunciation should take account of a number of specific features:

  • Before a vowel, ch \ʃ\ in front of a consonant, ch \k\ in words of Greek origin one speaks ch before vowel as \k\.
    chef \ʃɛf\
    Chef
    chou \ʃu\
    Kohl
    chrétien \kretjɛ̃\
    Christian
    chœur \kœr\
    Chor
    écho \eko\
    Echo
    orchestre \ɔrkɛstr\
    Orchester
  • f, ff, ph will be \f\ exception: the f is not pronounced at cerf, nerf, clef. On the other hand, the singular bœuf, œuf f. At Liaison, f is spoken as \v\ gesprochen.

    Koffer

    Hunger
    phrase \fraz\
    Rate

    bœuf \bœf\

    9 years
  • g At the end of the word is mute.
    sang \sɑ̃\
    Blut
    long \lɔ̃\
    lang
  • The h is always mute. One distinguishes between h muet (stummes h) and h aspiré (behauchtes h).
    • The h muet has no influence on the pronunciation. It is treated like a vowel, i.e., it takes place Elision and Liaison.
      l'homme \lɔm\
      the man
      s'habiller \sabije\
      sich anziehen
      les‿herbes \lezɛrb\
      the herbs
    • The h aspiré is a voiceless consonant and influences the pronunciation of its surroundings. It prevents liaison and elimination. In the dictionary h aspiré is marked with a '.
      je hais \ʒəɛ\
      I hate
      le hasard \ləazar\
      the chance
      les halles \leal\
      the market halls
      la haine \laɛn\
      hate
  • s is always vocal between vowels \z\. Exception: after nasal vowel, s is voiceless \s\.
    oser \oze\
    wagen
    isolé \izɔle\
    isoliert
    chanson \ʃɑ̃sɔ̃\
    Lied
  • The Ending ‑tie is used in words of Greek origin \si\ gesprochen.
    démocratie \demɔkrasi\
    Demokratie
    Exception: sortie \sɔrti\ Ausgang.
  • y is used alone or in a consonant environment \i\ in vocal surroundings, y \j\ or \ij\ gesprochen.
    physique \fizik\
    Physik
    tuyau \tɥijo\
    Rohr
