The Liaison

For the Liaison (binding) is the normally silent end consonant of a word with the louding vowel or stummen h This creates a new, flowing syllable.

les‿amis \le.za.mi\
the Friends
un‿ancien élève \œ̃.nɑ̃.sjɛ̃.ne.lɛv\
a former student
c'est‿un exemple \sɛ.tœ̃.nɛg.zɑ̃pl\
this is an example

The rules of the Liaison are complex and depend on grammatical and stylistic factors. One distinguishes between binding, forbidden and optional liaisons.

With a h aspiré (gehauchtes h) findet niemals The consonant is not bound.

les | haricots
the beans
les | héros
the heroes

The sounds of the Liaison

The most common binding sounds are:

  • \z\ after s, x, z (ca. 50% aller Liaisons)
    mes‿enfants
    meine Kinder
    dix‿ans
    10 years
    chez‿elle
    with her
  • \t\ after t, d (ca. 25% aller Liaisons)
    un petit‿oiseau
    a little bird
    quand‿on arrive
    when you arrive
    un grand‿arbre
    a big tree
  • \n\ after n (ca. 25% aller Liaisons)
    mon‿avis
    meine Meinung
    on‿a faim
    we are hungry
    un bon‿ami
    a good friend

More rarely, the following binding sounds occur:

  • \ʁ\ after r
    le premier‿avion
    the first aeroplane
    un léger‿incident
    a minor incident
  • \p\ after p (almost only after beaucoup and trop)
    beaucoup‿aimé
    very liked
    trop‿occupé
    too busy
  • \g\ very rare
    un long‿article
    a long article
  • \v\ after neuf (only before ans and heures)
    neuf‿ans
    9 years
    dix-neuf‿heures
    neunzehn Uhr

If an end consonant is always spoken in the single word, one speaks of enchaînement The consonant is simply "attached" to the next syllable.

avec \a.vɛk\ → avec‿elle \a.vɛ.kɛl\
with → with it
mille \mil\ → mille‿ans \mi.lɑ̃\
a thousand years
l'immeuble \i.mœbl\ → l'immeuble‿ancien \i.mœ.blɑ̃.sjɛ̃\
the building &rr; the old building

Binding liaisons (Liaisons obligatoires)

Liaison shall be binding in the following cases:

Within the verbal group

  • Pronouns + Verb
    Nous‿attendons ici.
    We'll wait here.
    Vous‿avez faim.
    You're hungry.
  • Pronomen + pronomen
    Ils‿en veulent.
    They want to get away with it.
  • Verb + pronouns (for Inversion)
    Est-il à la maison ?
    Is he home?
    Allons-y !
    Let's go!

Within the nominal group

  • Determinant/number + noun
    ces‿arbres
    these trees
    quelques‿amis
    einige Freunde
    trois‿euros
    drei Euro
  • Previous adjective + noun
    un petit‿appartement
    a small apartment
    de beaux‿yeux
    beautiful eyes
    un bon‿ami
    a good friend

With prepositions and adverbs

  • Single-syllable preposition + nouns
    en‿avion
    by plane
    dans‿un hôtel
    in a hotel
  • Single-syllable adverb + adjective
    très‿intéressant
    very interesting
    plus‿efficace
    effizienter

Special cases

  • After quand, dont
    Quand‿on est prêt...
    When you're ready...
    C'est la femme dont‿il est amoureux.
    That's the woman he's in love with.
  • In fixed phrases and phrases:
    • de temps‿en temps (from time to time)
    • tout‿à coup (suddenly)
    • c'est-à-dire (meaning)
    • les États-Unis (the United States)
    • petit‿à petit (by and by)
    • plus‿ou moins (more or less)

Verbotene Liaisons (Liaisons interdites)

Liaison should not be formed in the following cases:

  • Noun
    Mon chat | aime dormir.
    My cat likes to sleep.
    Les enfants | arrivent.
    The kids are arriving.
  • According to the question pronoun for inversion: Verb -- Pronouns
    Voulez-vous | un café ?
    Would you like some coffee?
  • Noun (singular)
    un train | direct
    a direct train
    un projet | important
    an important project
  • Before huit, onze, oui
    les | huit participants
    the eight participants
    les | onze joueurs
    the eleven players
    Il a répondu deux | oui et un non à mes questions.
    He answered my questions with two yes and one no.
  • After et
    un stylo et | un cahier
    a pen and a notebook
  • After Question Adverbs (except in the case of exceptions)
    Combien | en reste-t-il ?
    How many are left?
    Comment | est-il venu ?
    How did he get here?
    Ausnahmen:
    Comment‿allez-vous ?
    How are you?
    Quand‿est-ce que...?
    Wann...?

Optional but frequent liaisons

Some liaisons are optional, but are very often made in the spoken language and are therefore recommended:

  • After the Hilfsverb être
    Je suis‿arrivé.
    I've arrived.
    Il est‿entré.
    He's come in.
  • Between the verb être and the following attribute
    C'est‿amusant.
    That's funny.
    Je suis‿heureux.
    I'm happy.
