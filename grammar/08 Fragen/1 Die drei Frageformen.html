The three types of questions

In French, there are three main ways of asking questions:

  1. Intonationsfrage: Luc va au bureau ? ↗
  2. est-ce que‑Frage: Est-ce que Luc va au bureau ?
  3. Inversionsfrage: Luc va-t-il au bureau ?

Intonationsfrage

The German does not know the question of intonation. However, it is often used in spoken French and is very easy to form.

The intonation question retains the position of the sentence members of the statement, but is spoken with increasing intonation, e.g.:

Mathieu va au café ? ↗
Does Mathieu go to the café?

est-ce que‑Frage

The question with est-ce que is formed by: est-ce que The position of the individual sentence members in the sentence remains unchanged. est-ce que does not exist in German. It is used in both the spoken and the written language.

Before vowel and stummem h will est-ce que to est-ce qu’.

Est-ce que tu vas au bureau ?
Are you going to the office?
Est-ce qu’on va au cinéma ce soir ?
Shall we go to the movies tonight?

Inversionsfrage

The question of inversion is quite similar to that of German. However, it is not used very often in spoken French. It is mainly found in written texts, e.g. in letters.

The inversion question with and without question word is formed as follows:

Quand pars-tu en vacances ?
Comment vas-tu en vacances ?
Comment va-t-il ?
Où habite-t-elle ?
Veux-tu prendre le train ?

In the inversion question, the Subjektpronomen behind the verb. A hyphen is inserted between verb and subject. The question words are before the verb when inversion questions are asked.

In the 3rd person singular at il, elle or on enters between verb and subject pronouns ‑t-if the verb form is on ‑e or ‑a endet.

Chante-t‑elle bien ?
Is she singing well?

If the object of the sentence consists of a noun, the thing becomes more complicated, because a noun cannot stand behind the verb.

If you want to form the inversion question and a noun is a sentence object, the statement is retained and the corresponding subject pronoun is attached to the verb with the help of a hyphen.

Claire habite-t-elle à Lyon ?
Wohnt Claire in Lyon?
Monsieur Dubois connaît-il l’Allemagne ?
Kennt Herr Dubois Deutschland?
